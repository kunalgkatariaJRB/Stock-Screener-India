Principles

Drawn from Graham, Buffett, Munger, and Naval Ravikant.

Click any to expand

  1. i

    Mr. Market is your servant, not your guide.

    The market quotes prices every day. Those quotes reflect emotion as much as analysis. We buy when he is depressed, we sell when he is manic, otherwise we ignore him.

    — Graham, The Intelligent Investor
  2. ii

    A wonderful business at a fair price > a fair business at a wonderful price.

    The cheapest stock is rarely the best one. Quality compounds over time; cheapness without quality is a trap. We start with return on capital, not the price tag.

    — Buffett (1989 Letter)
  3. iii

    Margin of safety is a habit, not a slogan.

    We require a real gap between what something is worth and what it costs. Estimates are imperfect; the gap protects us from our imperfections.

    — Graham, central thesis
  4. iv

    Be fearful when others are greedy, and greedy when others are fearful.

    The biggest gains come from buying against a gloomy crowd. Crowd sentiment is a contrary indicator at extremes.

    — Buffett · refined by Naval
  5. v

    Time horizons are measured in cycles, not quarters.

    Compounding is the only force that turns small bets into large outcomes — and it requires sitting still through ugly quarters. Three to five years is the minimum.

    — Buffett · echoed by Naval
  6. vi

    Play long-term games with long-term people.

    Trust, reputation, and compounding all benefit from continuity. A great management team is almost always a multi-decade hold.

    — Naval Ravikant
  7. vii

    Stay inside your circle of competence.

    Investing in businesses you don't understand is gambling. Our verdicts are confined to industries we can read.

    — Buffett & Munger
  8. viii

    Concentration with conviction; diversification against ignorance.

    Six to ten ideas you understand deeply will outperform forty you barely follow. Diversify across sectors, not within one.

    — Buffett's portfolio in practice
  9. ix

    Sell when the thesis breaks — not when the price falls.

    A 30% drawdown on a healthy compounder is a chance to add. A 30% drawdown on a broken story is a warning.

    — Munger's discipline
  10. x

    Position size is the only real risk control.

    No single bet, however brilliant, justifies a position that could materially impair the family's capital. Size for the worst case, not the best.

    — Taleb's first lesson
